## Context

Yayasan Ishlahul Ummah is an Islamic education foundation in Bogor operating
both SDIT and SMPIT schools. Its website needed to do more than publish school
information. It had to establish trust with parents, express the institution's
character, explain two education levels without losing a shared identity, and
make the route to admission easy to find.

The content was broad: the foundation's history and leadership, curriculum,
facilities, extracurricular activities, testimonials, admissions requirements,
and contact information. The design challenge was to give that density a clear
rhythm instead of reducing it to a stack of institutional text.

## Approach

I designed the experience around three connected layers:

1. **A recognizable visual identity.** Deep maroon and warm gold translate the
   foundation's existing identity into a contemporary digital palette. Large
   sans-serif headlines, selective serif italics, documentary photography, and
   generous spacing make the site feel confident without losing warmth.
2. **A parent-oriented content architecture.** The site separates the
   foundation profile, SDIT, SMPIT, gallery, and contact information into clear
   routes while keeping admissions calls to action visible throughout. The
   homepage works as both an introduction and a guided path into deeper detail.
3. **Motion with a job to do.** Staged text reveals, rotating hero language,
   marquees, program tabs, and testimonial controls add energy and help pace a
   long page. The interactions support hierarchy and orientation rather than
   competing with the school content.

The responsive system adapts the same editorial composition for smaller
screens with compact navigation, stacked calls to action, fluid typography,
and touch-friendly controls.

## Evidence

- Six core visitor surfaces: home, profile, SDIT, SMPIT, gallery, and contact
- Dedicated descriptions for each school level within one shared visual system
- Persistent SPMB messaging and repeated routes from interest to inquiry
- Filterable gallery, program tabs, testimonial controls, and embedded location
- Indonesian-language metadata and responsive layouts for the intended audience

## Outcome

The live deployment gives the foundation a cohesive, presentation-ready web
presence and gives stakeholders a concrete surface for reviewing the final
content and assets. It also demonstrates how I approach client web work: learn
the institution's identity, organize what visitors need to know, then use
typography, composition, and motion to make that information feel intentional.

The project is still being refined. The next phase is to replace remaining
provisional content, complete stakeholder review, and measure how effectively
the admissions paths turn visits into inquiries.
