## Context

RFM segmentation gave the marketing team a useful view of customer value, but
not enough context to personalize a campaign. Two customers could share the
same recency, frequency, and monetary profile while differing substantially in
age, income, household structure, and response behavior.

That limitation was visible in a dataset of 2,240 retail customers. The six RFM
tiers identified large commercial groups such as 712 Champions and 598 Can't
Lose customers. Yet their demographic composition varied in ways the labels did
not explain. Generation and RFM tier were statistically related
(`χ² = 101.49`, `p < 0.001`), but the small effect size (`Cramér's V = 0.11`)
showed that RFM alone captured little of the demographic variation.

The business question therefore changed from **"Who is valuable?"** to **"What
kind of valuable customer is this, and how should we engage them?"**

## Approach

I developed the analysis in three stages:

1. **Build a reliable customer view.** I audited missing values, duplicates,
   invalid records, zero-variance fields, and extreme outliers. After removing
   45 implausible observations, I engineered age, generation, household
   structure, customer tenure, campaign engagement, total purchases, total
   spending, spend per purchase, and RFM scores.
2. **Select features for distinct business signals.** Pearson correlation and
   Cramér's V exposed redundant inputs. I retained 11 mixed-type features that
   balanced independence with commercial relevance: RFM tier, generation,
   income band, campaign response, parent and widow status, recency score, age,
   engagement rate, tenure, and spend per purchase.
3. **Find stable groups in mixed data.** UMAP created a joint representation of
   numerical and categorical behavior, then HDBSCAN identified dense customer
   groups without requiring a preset number of clusters. A 300-trial Optuna
   search tuned the density parameters against DBCV, a clustering-quality
   metric designed for irregular cluster shapes.

The selected model produced **eight clusters with 100% customer coverage and a
DBCV score of 0.73**. That combination indicated well-separated groups without
discarding customers as noise.

## Evidence

The new groups preserved the commercial signal from RFM while adding the
demographic context required for differentiated messaging:

- **High-value loyalists (35.0%)** split into two Champion groups: older Baby
  Boomers with median age 66 and income of $62,000, and Generation X customers
  with median age 49 and income of $62,882.
- **Reactivation audiences (40.0%)** separated Can't Lose customers into an
  older, higher-income group (median age 65; income $56,129) and a younger
  Generation X group (median age 47; income $46,098).
- **New-customer audiences (12.6%)** distinguished Baby Boomers from Generation
  X customers, revealing different income and life-stage profiles behind the
  same RFM label.
- **Emerging value (5.8%)** isolated a lower-income Generation X group whose
  dominant RFM profile was Promising rather than combining it with established
  high-value customers.
- **Younger champions (6.6%)** surfaced as a predominantly Millennial,
  non-parent group with a median age of 36, a profile hidden inside the broader
  Champion tier.

Campaign behavior added a clear timing signal. Recent high spenders, defined as customers
who had purchased within 14 days and spent between $1,253 and $2,525, showed a
56% response rate. Among similarly high-spending customers whose last purchase
was 19–39 days earlier, response fell to 42%; for customers inactive for 79–99
days, the best-performing spend band reached only 18%.

## Outcome

The analysis turned six broad value tiers into eight addressable audiences and
a more practical campaign framework:

- Protect the two Champion groups with loyalty benefits and premium cross-sell,
  while adapting creative to their different generations.
- Treat the two Can't Lose groups as separate reactivation programs rather than
  sending one generic win-back offer.
- Give new customers cohort-specific onboarding and measure movement into
  higher-value groups over time.
- Prioritize recent high spenders when campaign responsiveness is strongest,
  then change the offer or channel as recency declines.

This project produced a targeting strategy, not a claim of incremental revenue.
The source is a public customer-personality dataset, and customer lifetime value
was not available. Before production use, the segment definitions should be
tested for stability on newer data and their campaign recommendations validated
through controlled experiments.
