## Context

Two providers serve the same underlying model (GLM-5.2) through different
infrastructure, pricing, and rate limits. From a user's perspective, are they
actually equivalent? A model is only as good as the infrastructure serving it,
and "same model" doesn't guarantee same output, same speed, or same cost.

## Approach

The harness measures four dimensions that matter to real users:

1. **Latency**: TTFT, inter-token, and end-to-end, reported as p50/p95. Two
   populations are tracked: clean (no throttling) for apples-to-apples
   inference speed, and effective (including backoff) for real-world experience
   under load.

2. **Parity**: Cross-provider answer disagreement at temperature=0. The
   headline number for "do these providers produce the same output?" If
   parity drops, one of them is silently modifying the model (quantization,
   system prompt injection, or different model version).

3. **Cost**: Supports both metered (per-token) and subscription (flat-fee)
   pricing models. Subscription plans are translated into an effective
   $/1M-token range using configurable usage-intensity tiers.

4. **Rate limits**: 429s, backoff time, and retry counts per provider,
   treated as a first-class signal rather than noise. A provider that
   throttles aggressively may be cheaper per token but more expensive per
   successful request.

## Evidence

- Standard evaluation suites: MMLU, GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, HumanEval
- p50/p95 latency reporting with clean vs effective populations
- Cross-provider parity measurement at temperature=0
- Cost modeling for both metered and subscription pricing
- Rate-limit behavior tracked as first-class signal (429s, backoff, retries)
- JSON + Markdown report output

## Outcome

GLMsBench answers a question that providers don't want you to ask: when two
services claim to serve the same model, which one actually delivers? The answer
is rarely "they're identical," and the differences show up in latency, output
parity, and reliability long before they show up in the price.
